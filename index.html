Section 01 — the instrument

Four pillars × five scales = twenty cells

Each cell carries two documented weights that sum to 1: how much it counts toward PITO (products in, trash out — the extractive side) and toward DIDO (data in, data out — the capacity side). The colour you see is not those weights. It is the reading currently attached to that cell — red when the evidence says extractive, green when it says regenerative. Section 02 — reading the colour

Three ways a cell gets its colour — and why they must never look alike

A live feed, a number from a report, and somebody's honest guess are three different kinds of knowledge. An instrument that renders them identically is lying by design. So each is drawn differently, and each says what it is on the cell face.

How a raw number becomes a 0–1 reading

This is the step that has to be auditable, because it is where judgement enters. Every indicator declares its direction and its normalisation up front.

Direction

Some indicators mean the opposite of others. DIDO means a bigger raw number is better — more repairs, more open datasets. PITO means a bigger raw number is worse — more tonnes imported, more days of delay. PITO-direction indicators are inverted when normalised, so that on screen green always means regenerative, never "big number".

Normalisation

Raw values are scaled into 0–1 against a stated reference — a peer group, a science-based target, or a practical ceiling. Following Boeing's discipline: priority × self-sufficiency where formal data exists, a documented proxy where it does not, flagged as such. The reference is written next to every indicator in the cell panel, so you can disagree with it specifically.

Upload raw values, never pre-normalised scores. If you convert 143 repairs to 0.7 in your own spreadsheet, nobody downstream can see how you got there — and the reading stops being auditable.

Section 03b — team submissions

Load a team's data into the matrix

Workshop teams put a CSV in data/submissions/ of this repository. This panel reads those files and turns each row into a cell reading — the same normalisation the rest of the instrument uses, with the same provenance rules. Nothing is invented: a row without a source or an observation date is rejected and told why.

Network snapshots — the sources the network already owns

A scheduled job (.github/workflows/snapshots.yml) fetches fablabs.io and Smart Citizen weekly and commits dated JSON into data/snapshots/ — git history is the provenance ledger. Loading them here turns each into a static reading: a real value, frozen at its observation date, with its normalisation stated in the file. Needs the page served over http.

Move a session between machines

Readings live in this browser only. Export everything — readings, workshop cards, your ρ estimate — as one JSON file; import it on another machine, or merge several tables' files after a workshop. Importing never deletes: existing readings are only overwritten by a newer observation of the same cell, and the report says exactly what happened.

Section 05 — pilots

Where each city sits, and which way it is heading

PITO runs left to right: how much the city still buys far and wastes near. DIDO runs bottom to top: how much it can sense, share and make locally. The top-left corner is the target. Paris and Hamburg are reconstructed points from single-cell measurements, shown to locate the ceiling — not new readings. The dashed red curve is every position that scores exactly the old 37/100 at ρ = 1; the purple point is this dashboard's own city — fitted from your attached evidence, or driven by the simulation sliders — with its ring sized by ρ.

Section 06 — the architecture underneath

The seven layers of the Fab City Full Stack

Not a ladder — seven interdependent layers, from the machines in the lab to the knowledge the network shares back. Layers 1–4 are operational, closest to direct action. Layer 5 governs. Layers 6–7 are the boundary and horizon the rest has to fit inside. The matrix is what you get when you fold this vertical architecture against territory.

Diez, Charny & Kohtala (2024), The Fab City Full Stack, Fab City Foundation.

Section 07 — WS3, FAB26 Boston

Why this room, and why now

Why the exercise happens on the bottom row

This is the part worth saying out loud, because it is the difference between a workshop that feels like homework and one that feels like power. We audited every indicator in the framework and asked a blunt question: who can actually produce this number?

The network owns the Community row and almost nothing above it. So the exercise puts you where you have sovereignty — and then shows you the network sources that are the honest route upward. You are not being asked to guess about the planet. You are being asked what you could pull this month, and who you would have to email for the rest.

Section 09 — named honestly

What this instrument does not yet know

Naming these in the body of the methodology is the price of the credibility the instrument asks for. Every one of them is from the FCI 3.0 methodology's own open-gaps section, or from the evidence review behind it.

    Where each number in this dashboard comes from

    Two provenance classes, marked on every cell.

    documented

    Weights and notes published on the FCI 3.0 methodology page. Six cells: the Generation 1+2 pair (Economic × City and × Region), the three flagged for review (Economic × Bioregion, Environmental × Community, Economic × Community), and the two thinnest governance cells.

    reconstructed

    Proposed here from the Full Stack Metrics Framework FAB26 working paper [Vivanco 2024] to fill cells the v0 table has not yet published. A working proposal for discussion — not canon, and clearly marked everywhere it appears.

    Three weight tables in the wild — the canonisation gap

    The FCI 3.0 site publishes a v0 table (12 cells), this dashboard carries a documented + reconstructed set, and the exhibition console ships its own full 20-cell table. They agree on of 20 cells. Until a versioned weights.json becomes the single source every surface consumes, each new surface deepens the drift. The machine-readable comparison lives at data/weights.json in this repository.
